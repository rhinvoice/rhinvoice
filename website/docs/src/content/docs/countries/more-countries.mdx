Rhinvoice is built for **more countries** from the start. Everything that differs between countries lives in **one country profile per country**: dated VAT rates, legal notices, mandatory details, payment QR code, number format and the official sources. The rest of Rhinvoice only asks the profile and names no country. A new country, for example the **Netherlands**, is therefore a new profile, not a change throughout the program.

<Aside type="tip" title="Customers abroad work today">
Your **company's** country must be Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Germany or Austria. Your **customers** can be anywhere: Rhinvoice chooses the tax treatment from the company's and the customer's country (domestic, reverse charge, intra-Community supply, export) and prints the matching notice.
</Aside>

## What a country profile holds

- the VAT law: the EU VAT Directive or the Swiss MWSTG
- the VAT area (Switzerland, for example, together with Liechtenstein)
- the **VAT rates with the date each applies from**, so that older invoices are right too
- the words on documents in German and English: the tax's name, the label of the VAT number, the **legal notice** per tax treatment and the greeting in e-mails
- the **mandatory details** Rhinvoice checks before issuing
- number format, **payment QR code** (Swiss QR-bill or EPC QR code), default currency and whether rounding to 0.05 is offered
- the **official sources** of all of it

The interface shows tax names, rates and fields from the profile, so it needs hardly any change for a new country.

## Example: the Netherlands

The developer guide walks through the work with the Netherlands (`NL`) as the example:

<Steps>

1. **Add the country code.**
2. **Write the profile**, as a copy of the German or Austrian one, since both are EU countries: dated rates, notices in both languages, mandatory details, payment code and sources.
3. **Register the profile**, so that Rhinvoice knows it.
4. **Add demo data**, with official example IBANs, never with real accounts.
5. **Offer the country in the interface.**
6. **Check**: tests say whether the profile is complete and what is missing. Finally issue an invoice and look at the PDF.

</Steps>

Since EU countries share the logic of the VAT Directive, a new EU country with the euro needs no change to the tax logic. The Netherlands pay in euros, so the EPC QR code fits there too.

## What makes a country bigger

Some things are not in a profile yet. They decide how much work a country is:

- **A currency of its own.** Rhinvoice knows CHF and EUR. A country with its own currency (for example PLN or SEK) needs it added first.
- **Another language.** Documents are written in German or English. If customers expect their own language, a new language with all its texts comes first.
- **A national e-invoice network**, such as Italy's. Every invoice already contains ZUGFeRD / Factur-X following EN 16931 ([e-invoices](/docs/invoices/e-invoice/)); a mandatory network would be a module of its own.
- **VAT law outside the EU.** A country with its own VAT law needs its own rules for the tax treatments.

## How your country gets in

1. **Ask for it:** open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/rhinvoice/rhinvoice/issues/new/choose) with the *Tax or legal rule* form. Name the country and link the official sources: the VAT law, the rates and what an invoice must state.
2. **Build it:** developers follow the guide [Adding a country](https://github.com/rhinvoice/rhinvoice/blob/main/docs/adding-a-country.md). More under [For developers](/docs/help/developers/).
3. **Contribute it:** the country comes in by pull request, with the sources and a PDF of an example invoice.

<Aside type="caution" title="No tax advice">
Rhinvoice gives no tax advice. Every rule in Rhinvoice names its official source, so that it can be checked.
</Aside>
